What actually happens to your sample

Pick a test, watch it run step by step and drag to look around the equipment. No engineering background needed.

Standard Penetration Test IS 2131

What it checksMeasures how dense or stiff the soil is at different depths, so the foundation can be designed for the right bearing capacity.

  1. 01

    A borehole is drilled to the test depth and cleaned.

  2. 02

    A split-spoon sampler on drill rods is driven by a 63.5 kg hammer falling 750 mm.

  3. 03

    Blows are counted for three 150 mm steps; the first step only seats the sampler.

  4. 04

    N-value = blows for the last 300 mm. The soil caught in the sampler goes to our lab.

What the result tells you

Low N-values mean soft or loose soil (deeper or wider foundations); high values mean dense soil or rock. N-values feed directly into the safe bearing capacity in your report.

Plate load test IS 1888

What it checksMeasures how much the ground actually settles under load, at the level where your footing will sit.

  1. 01

    A pit is excavated to foundation level and a steel test plate is bedded on the soil.

  2. 02

    A hydraulic jack pushes the plate down against a heavy loaded platform (kentledge).

  3. 03

    Load is increased in steps while dial gauges record the settlement.

  4. 04

    A load–settlement curve is plotted to work out the safe bearing pressure.

What the result tells you

The curve tells your structural engineer how much load the soil can carry while keeping settlement within safe limits.

Overview

Every foundation decision starts with reliable subsurface data. MPAD QC Lab runs its own rotary and tripod drilling rigs for borehole investigation on land and over water, carries out in-situ tests at site, and tests the recovered samples in our NABL-accredited laboratory at Hardware Park, Shamshabad.

The result is a single, engineer-ready geotechnical report: borehole logs, soil profile, ground-water level, laboratory results and clear recommendations on foundation type, depth and safe bearing capacity.

Frequently asked questions

How many boreholes does my site need?

It depends on plot size, building load and how variable the ground is. As a guide, small residential plots often need 2–3 boreholes while larger buildings need one every 30–50 m. We recommend the number and depth after seeing your layout.

What is SBC and why does my structural engineer need it?

Safe Bearing Capacity (SBC) is the load per unit area the soil can carry without shear failure or excessive settlement. Your engineer uses it to size footings or decide on piles, so an accurate SBC directly affects safety and foundation cost.

Can you investigate sites over water?

Yes. We have carried out marine boreholes from floating barges with SPT and sampling for water-front structures.

Do you carry out pile load and pile integrity tests?

Yes. We conduct vertical, lateral and pull-out load tests on test and working piles as per IS 2911 (Part 4), and low-strain pile integrity tests to check pile continuity.
