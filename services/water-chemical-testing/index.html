pH value IS 3025 (Pt 11)

What it checksChecks whether water used for mixing and curing concrete is too acidic, which can weaken concrete and corrode steel.

  1. 01

    A water sample is collected from your site source in a clean bottle.

  2. 02

    The pH meter is calibrated with standard buffer solutions.

  3. 03

    The probe is dipped in the sample and the reading is allowed to stabilise.

  4. 04

    The value is compared with the IS 456 requirement.

What the result tells you

IS 456 requires a pH of not less than 6. Lower values mean the water must be treated or replaced before use.

Chlorides IS 3025 (Pt 32)

What it checksMeasures chlorides in water — chlorides are the main cause of rusting of steel bars inside concrete.

  1. 01

    A measured volume of sample is taken in a flask with an indicator added.

  2. 02

    The burette is filled with silver nitrate solution.

  3. 03

    Silver nitrate is added drop by drop while swirling.

  4. 04

    The colour turns from yellow to brick-red at the end-point.

  5. 05

    Chloride content is calculated from the volume used.

What the result tells you

IS 456 limits chlorides to 500 mg/l for reinforced concrete (2000 mg/l for plain concrete). Higher values put the steel at risk.

Overview

IS 456 sets clear limits on pH, chlorides, sulphates and solids in water used for concrete. Bore-well and tanker water in and around Hyderabad can vary a lot, so testing your source protects every pour that follows.

For foundations, we test soil and ground-water chemistry so your designer can choose the right cement type and concrete cover.

Frequently asked questions

How much water sample is needed?

About 2 litres in a clean, tightly sealed plastic bottle, filled to the top and delivered within 24 hours of collection.

Is drinking water quality the same as water for concrete?

No. IS 456 uses its own limits for concrete. Some potable water can be unsuitable, and some non-potable water can be acceptable. Testing tells you which.
